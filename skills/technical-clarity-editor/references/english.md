# English module

- Prefer a short direct verb to an avoidable nominalization.
- Name the actor when responsibility matters.
- In procedures, use imperative mood and place the condition before the command.
- In strict mode, replace passive constructions with active voice.
- Avoid stacked auxiliaries, empty metacommentary, marketing adjectives, and unexplained phrasal verbs.
- Keep articles when they prevent ambiguity.
- Use the spelling and jurisdiction required by the project; do not impose American spelling globally.
- Do not ban contractions, semicolons, or phrasal verbs outside strict mode merely because they exist.
- Preserve defined legal terms and discipline-specific terminology.
- Use one canonical label for each controlled concept.
- Define a controlled term on first use when its project meaning differs from ordinary English.

Check especially:

- long noun stacks;
- ambiguous `it`, `this`, `that`, and `they`;
- passive voice that hides responsibility;
- conditionals whose exception is remote from the rule;
- `ensure`, `guarantee`, or causal verbs stronger than the evidence;
- different labels used for the same system, role, variable, or legal concept.
